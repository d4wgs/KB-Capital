What the target compounds to

The curve is shallow for months and steep at the end. Halfway through the year the compounded figure is about 6.1x, roughly a sixth of the final 37.8x. That shape only holds if the daily gains keep arriving without interruption, which is why consistency and capital turnover matter more to us than any single trade. One large loss resets the base that every later gain compounds on.

Process

  1. 01

    Source an edge and write the thesis

    We take positions only in markets where our own research gives us an informational advantage over the current price. Before entry we write a thesis that documents the methodology behind the trade. If we cannot write it down, we do not trade it.

  2. 02

    Entry criteria

    We trade the high-conviction, high-probability side only. The market must also be liquid enough, with a tight enough spread, that a one-cent move can be captured net of costs. A good thesis in an illiquid market is a pass.

  3. 03

    Position sizing and maximum drawdown

    Size and a predefined maximum drawdown are set before the order goes in. Both are measured against total fund capital (NAV), the same base as the 1% target.

  4. 04

    Monitoring and exit

    We aim to open and close within about an hour, which lets us watch the book against the drawdown limit and a profit and time window. We take the profit if the move comes, exit at the drawdown limit if it does not, and use a one-hour time stop on anything still open.

  5. 05

    Post-trade review

    Once a position is closed we compare the outcome with the thesis written before entry, whether the trade made or lost money.

Cash is a position. With no edge, there is no trade.

Why we scalp instead of holding to resolution

Where we hold an informational edge, a move from about 85% to 86% is generally easier and quicker to capture, with less risk, than a move from 98% to 99%.

At mid-range prices the market is still repricing as information arrives, so an edge has room to work. Near the extremes the market has already absorbed the information. The payoff is also highly skewed against the buyer: at 98% we would risk about 98 to make about 1 or 2. A single adverse resolution then erases many small wins, which is tail risk.

Risk

Fixed exits

Every position has a maximum drawdown and a time window defined before entry, so the exit is decided before the price starts moving.

Spread and liquidity

Our edge is measured in cents. If the spread or thin depth absorbs a one-cent move, the trade has no expected value and we do not take it.

Resolution and tail risk

Short holding periods reduce our exposure to event risk, but they do not remove it. A market can move sharply or resolve against us before our exit. The asymmetry described above is the reason we avoid paying 98 to make 1.

The target is not a guarantee

One percent a day is a target. Real results will include losing days, fees and slippage, and capital is at risk.
